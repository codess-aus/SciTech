Chapter 02 / The ocean

Below the surface.

The sea changes with every metre of depth, beginning with the light.

At the top of the image, sunlight breaks through the surface. Farther down, the water turns darker and the rays become faint.

Where the light goes

Water absorbs and scatters sunlight. Red wavelengths disappear quickly; blue light travels farther, helping give clear open water its blue appearance. Eventually there is too little sunlight for photosynthesis.

The plants near the bottom of this shallow illustrated scene need that light. In real oceans, most photosynthetic life is concentrated in the sunlit upper layer, while deeper ecosystems depend on food falling from above or on other energy sources.

More than empty blue

The fish at different depths hint at the many habitats hidden beneath a single surface. Temperature, pressure, food, and light all change with depth, and marine life has adapted to those changing conditions.

Look again

Trace the pale beams from the surface to the darker water. What might change for an animal as the light fades?
