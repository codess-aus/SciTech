Chapter 03 / The forest

Life between the trees.

A woodland is not one green space, but many overlapping places to live.

The trees in this picture frame a bright path, while overlapping leaves cast the ground into shade. Those layers make a forest more than a collection of trunks.

From canopy to ground

High branches form a canopy that catches much of the sunlight. Beneath it, smaller trees and shrubs grow in the filtered light. At ground level, shade-tolerant plants, fungi, and decomposers find different ways to thrive.

Where the canopy opens above the path, extra light reaches the forest floor. Even a small gap can change which seedlings grow there, shaping the next generation of trees.

Connected by change

Fallen leaves and wood become food for decomposers, which return nutrients to the soil. Roots take up water and nutrients, leaves capture energy from sunlight, and animals move among the layers. A forest is a living system in constant motion.

Look again

Compare the bright opening with the shaded edges. Imagine which plants might grow best in each place.
