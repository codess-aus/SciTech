Chapter 01 / The sky

A map in the stars.

For centuries, we have looked up and found stories written in points of light.

The bright points in this illustration seem to make a shape. Thin lines connect them, just as people have imagined patterns in the sky for thousands of years.

Patterns from our point of view

The stars in a constellation can be separated by enormous distances. They only look close together because we see them from one place: Earth. Move far enough away and the familiar shape would change.

The mountains at the bottom of the picture ground that view. As Earth turns, stars appear to travel across our sky. Over a year, the night side of Earth faces different directions, bringing different constellations into view.

A useful kind of story

Long before modern telescopes, people used these patterns to remember where to look, mark seasons, and navigate. Today astronomers still use named regions of the sky to locate objects, even though the connecting lines are imaginary.

Look again

Find the five brightest connected stars. The shape belongs to our viewpoint; each star has its own place in space.
